Junior RF Engineer · Mobile Communications America
Jan 2026 – Aug 2026 ▾
  • Public Safety (ERRCS) DAS Design — Owned the end-to-end iBwave design workflow for in-building public safety coverage systems across a wide range of facility types including apartments, schools, colleges, stadiums, hotels, and data centers. Each project started with importing architectural floor plans then building a precise 3D model in AutoCAD — scaling every floor, geolocating the structure, and drawing wall materials and structural details to create an accurate RF propagation environment. Identified the optimal donor tower using Radio Reference and confirmed line-of-sight and elevation profiles through Google Earth. Documented the selected tower including antenna type, control channel frequencies, and public safety bands. Built the full system in iBwave using Comba and ADRF components — creating the signal source, placing antennas, and routing the distribution network with couplers, tappers, and splitters to achieve code-compliant coverage throughout the building.
  • Cellular DAS Design — Designed LTE and 5G in-building distributed antenna systems for AT&T, T-Mobile, and Verizon. After completing the building model, reviewed each project request to determine which carriers and frequency bands were required. Selected and configured the appropriate headend platform — including JMA Wireless and NG Comba Flex — based on project scope and carrier preferences. Built the full downstream distribution network running iBwave coverage simulations iteratively until signal levels met all carrier acceptance thresholds across every area of the facility.
  • Onsite RF Testing & Verification — Conducted field testing at various site types using PCTEL scanning receivers and TEMS testing software to validate in-building coverage after system installation. Collected and documented LTE/5G KPIs including PUCCH, PCI, EARFCN, RSRP, RSRQ, SINR, and throughput data across multiple floors and spaces. Analyzed results to identify areas of signal degradation or coverage gaps and communicated findings to the project team for remediation.
  • RF Component Familiarity — Developed hands-on understanding of passive components (splitters, couplers, tappers, combiners, directional and omni antennas) and active components (BDAs, repeaters, amplifiers, fiber-based DAS systems) — learning how component selection and placement decisions directly affect system performance and compliance.
Electrical Team Member · Dallas Formula Racing EV — UTD
Aug 2024 – Dec 2025 ▾
  • PCB Design for Vehicle Systems — Designed and implemented multiple PCBs for the 2026 Formula-style EV production car, including a 5V-to-12V boost converter for onboard power management, a rear taillight LED assembly, and the Brake System Plausibility Device (BSPD) — a safety-critical circuit required by FSAE regulations to detect simultaneous hard braking and throttle application. Each board went through the full design cycle from requirements review, schematic capture, and LTspice simulation through component selection, KiCad layout, and design review with senior leads.
  • Tools & Design Workflow — Used LTspice for circuit-level simulation to validate behavior before layout, KiCad for schematic capture and PCB layout, and Altium Designer for more complex board work — applying proper design rules, clearances, and layer stack planning to meet the electrical and mechanical constraints of a racecar environment.
  • Wire Harness & System Integration — Collaborated with senior electrical leads on wire harness design — understanding how individual PCBs integrate into the broader electrical architecture of the vehicle, routing decisions, connector selection, and ensuring the harness met the weight and packaging requirements of a Formula-style EV chassis.
